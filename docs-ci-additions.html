Critical Infrastructure Protection
and Compliance Framework

Kenya's Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act 2018, as amended by the 2024 Cyber Security Regulations, designates specific sectors as critical infrastructure and imposes the highest criminal penalties in Kenyan cyber law on organisations operating within them. The RETACH CRQ Engine models combined DPA + CMCA fine exposure for all CI sectors.

CMCA 2018/2024 s.18 — Maximum Fine
KES 25M
For unauthorised access to, interference with, or sabotage of critical infrastructure systems. Enforced by DCI and NC4 (National Computer and Cyberspace Centre).
DPA 2019 — Maximum Fine (concurrent)
KES 5M
Data Protection Act 2019 fines apply to all CI operators that process personal data. Enforced by ODPC. Imposed in addition to, not instead of, CMCA penalties.
Combined Regulatory Ceiling
KES 30M
DPA and CMCA penalties can be imposed simultaneously for the same incident. CMCA s.18 also carries a custodial sentence of up to 20 years imprisonment.
Legal basis: Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act No. 5 of 2018, Section 18 — as amended by the Cyber Security Regulations 2024 (Legal Notice No. __/2024). DPA 2019 applies independently and runs concurrently. An organisation can be prosecuted under both statutes for the same security failure. This is not a planning assumption — it is the current statutory position. Source: Kenya Law · NC4 · ODPC.

This does not constitute legal advice. RETACH recommends validation by a qualified Kenyan advocate for compliance decisions.

CRQ Engine — Critical Infrastructure σ Constant
All CI sub-sectors use a lognormal dispersion constant of σ = 1.10 in the RETACH Poisson-Lognormal frequency-severity model. This is the highest σ value in the engine — above Fintech (σ = 0.95) and SACCO DT (σ = 0.82). The elevated constant reflects: (1) nation-state and advanced persistent threat (APT) exposure; (2) OT/IT convergence risk where IT breaches can trigger physical consequences; (3) mandatory incident reporting obligations that increase observable frequency; and (4) high consequence cascading failures (e.g. power grid failure triggering banking downtime).

Calibration sources: Serianu Africa Cybersecurity Report 2023 (CI chapter) · Zurich Insurance Emerging Markets CI Loss Data · NC4 Kenya Incident Data · ENISA (EU Agency for Cybersecurity) CI Threat Landscape 2023 · IBM Cost of a Data Breach 2023 (critical infrastructure sector).

As with all RETACH σ constants: this is a calibrated estimate pending accumulation of RETACH Risk Ledger™ CI engagement data. It will be refined as CI engagements close.
