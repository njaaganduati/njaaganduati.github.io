CRQ Methodology

Version 1.1 · May 2026 · RETACH Digital Ltd

The RETACH Cyber Risk Quantification Engine applies a frequency-severity actuarial model to produce financial loss estimates for organisations assessed under the S.I.N.S. Framework™. This document describes the full methodology — the model steps, the assumptions behind every constant, and the sources used to calibrate them.

Plain-English Summary Every number the CRQ Engine produces traces back to a calculation described on this page. We use the same core mathematics as insurance actuaries — Expected Loss = Frequency × Severity — applied to your specific sector, size, and control posture. We then model the distribution of possible outcomes using lognormal statistics to show you not just the average year but a bad year and a very bad year.

How the model works — five steps

01
Count how often attacks happen (Frequency)
Base attack probabilities for six scenario types — phishing, ransomware, financial fraud, data breach, business continuity failure, and insider threat — are drawn from published East Africa sector incident data. A sector multiplier adjusts for the relative attack frequency of the assessed organisation's industry: a Fintech faces attacks 2.4× more often than baseline; a SACCO 1.8×. A risk modifier derived from the organisation's control posture (MFA adoption, backup posture, ODPC status, S.I.N.S. score) adjusts the final likelihood downward where controls are in place.
02
Estimate how much each event costs (Severity)
Financial impact per event is modelled as a percentage of annual revenue — the most reliable proxy for data volume, transaction scale, and operational complexity. A sector impact multiplier adjusts for the relative cost severity of the assessed sector: Fintechs pay 1.8× more per breach event than baseline, reflecting transaction losses and regulatory penalties. A staff/endpoint scaling factor applies a modest upward adjustment for larger organisations. The same control posture modifier that reduces likelihood also reduces severity.
03
Calculate Expected Annual Loss
Expected Annual Loss per scenario = annual probability × financial impact per event. Total EAL is the sum across all six scenarios. This is textbook actuarial frequency-severity — identical to the formula an insurance underwriter uses to price a premium.
EAL = Σ (Frequency × Severity) across all six S.I.N.S. scenarios
04
Model the loss distribution (Percentile Bands)
Losses are not smooth. Some years are quiet; some are catastrophic. The CRQ Engine models this skew using a lognormal distribution — the same statistical shape used by insurers for catastrophe loss modelling — because cyber losses have a long tail. The dispersion parameter (σ) is calibrated by sector: Fintechs have higher variability (σ=0.95) than County Governments (σ=0.65). This produces three outputs: the median year (50th percentile ≈ EAL), an above-average loss year (75th percentile), and a severe event year (95th percentile — a 1-in-20 outcome).
95th percentile = exp(ln(EAL) + 1.645 × σ) · Use for insurance sizing and board stress testing
05
Apply regulatory and coverage outputs
Regulatory fine exposure is modelled separately from EAL — it is a deterministic calculation based on identified compliance gaps and the applicable penalty schedule (ODPC: up to 5M KES or 1% turnover; SASRA; CBK). The Coverage Adequacy Check compares the organisation's entered coverage limit against the 95th percentile loss figure to identify a gap or surplus — this is a planning tool, not insurance advice. The RTO/RPO Gap module compares stated recovery tolerance against sector-benchmarked recovery time data, quantifying the financial cost of the gap in revenue per hour.

Model assumptions and their limits

Every constant in the CRQ Engine is listed here with its source and its honest limitation. This is the complete set of assumptions behind every output the engine produces.

Threat actors evolve. Model will be updated as RETACH engagement data accumulates.
Severity as % of revenue Impact calibrated as a share of annual revenue — larger organisations face larger absolute losses per event Actual severity depends on systems held, data volume, and attacker sophistication — not size alone. Organisations with disproportionate data assets relative to revenue may be underestimated.

Currency conversion USD equivalent shown at a fixed reference rate Exchange rate movements are not modelled. Local currency figures are the primary output. USD equivalents are indicative only.

CRQ Terms of Use

Effective May 2026 · RETACH Digital Ltd

These terms govern the use of all outputs produced by the RETACH CRQ Engine, S.I.N.S. Scanner, and associated tools at retach.tech and retach.io. By using these tools, you accept these terms.

1. Nature of outputs
CRQ outputs are financial planning estimates — not certifications
All figures produced by the RETACH CRQ Engine — including Expected Annual Loss, percentile loss bands, regulatory fine exposure estimates, coverage adequacy checks, and RTO/RPO gap figures — are financial planning estimates based on the actuarial model described in the Methodology section above. They are not: actuarial certifications, legal opinions, insurance advice, regulatory compliance certifications, or binding loss projections. They should be used for internal risk management, board reporting, and informed conversations with insurers, legal counsel, and regulators — not as standalone compliance documents or as the sole basis for insurance purchasing decisions.
2. Permitted use
Internal risk management and professional advisory
CRQ outputs may be used for: internal board and executive reporting; informing conversations with cyber insurers and brokers; scoping remediation investment decisions; supporting regulatory submissions as supplementary risk evidence. CRQ outputs may not be used for: marketing or advertising claims about security posture without qualification; as the primary basis for public statements about organisational risk; redistribution as standalone certified risk assessments without RETACH engagement.
3. Liability and disclaimer
RETACH Digital Ltd is not a licensed insurer, broker, or legal firm
RETACH Digital Ltd provides risk quantification and advisory services. We are not a licensed insurer, insurance broker, actuarial firm, or legal firm. Nothing produced by the CRQ Engine constitutes insurance advice, legal advice, or actuarial certification. Actual losses may be higher or lower than modelled figures. RETACH Digital Ltd accepts no liability for decisions made solely on the basis of CRQ Engine outputs without professional engagement. For insurance purchasing decisions, consult a licensed insurance broker. For regulatory compliance, consult qualified legal counsel.
4. Data and privacy
No client data is stored by the CRQ Engine
The RETACH CRQ Engine runs entirely in your browser. No inputs — revenue, staff count, sector, S.I.N.S. scores — are transmitted to or stored by RETACH servers. The OSINT and threat intelligence features make outbound requests to RETACH's API proxy (api.retach.io), which fetches publicly available threat data. No personally identifiable information is required or collected by any RETACH tool. If you choose to engage RETACH for a paid assessment, data handling is governed by the engagement letter issued at the start of that engagement.

The S.I.N.S. Framework™

The S.I.N.S. Framework™ for Digital Resilience is RETACH's proprietary methodology for structured assessment, quantification, and remediation of digital risk. It organises the complete digital risk surface of an organisation into four pillars, each assessed independently and combined into a composite posture score.

Four pillars
S¹ · Systems
Identity & Access
Asset inventory, identity governance, MFA, account lifecycle, endpoint control, patch management. Phishing and insider threat EAL.
S² · Infrastructure
Backup & DR
Backup posture, DR planning, RTO/RPO, server hardening, cloud configuration. Ransomware and business continuity EAL.
S³ · Network
Perimeter & Segmentation
Firewall posture, network segmentation, remote access controls, monitoring and detection. Fraud and lateral movement EAL.
S⁴ · Security
Governance & Policy
IS policy framework, incident response, compliance posture (DPA, SASRA, CBK, CMCA), staff awareness. Regulatory fine and breach EAL.
The three-stage delivery model Assess — structured review across all four pillars: Health Scan, AD Audit, Pentest, Systems Diagnostic.
Quantify — financial risk position from the CRQ Engine: EAL, percentile bands, coverage adequacy, RTO/RPO gap.
Remediate — controls implemented, tools deployed, posture sustained. Every engagement closes with a Risk Delta Report.
